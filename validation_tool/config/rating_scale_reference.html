Rating-scale reference

For each moment, you'll be asked: "At this moment, how well does the visual support what's being said?" Use this scale. Judge what the learner experiences — there is no need to count seconds or estimate timings.

5 — Very well

The visual fully supports the narration at this moment. The learner can integrate what they see with what they hear effortlessly; there is no cognitive gap between the two streams.

4 — Well

The visual largely supports the narration. The learner can connect the two with only minor effort — e.g. a brief glance back to the visual, or a small mental bridge that does not interrupt comprehension.

3 — Adequately

The pairing works, but the learner must hold one stream in working memory while waiting for — or looking for — the other. Comprehension is preserved, but at a noticeable cognitive cost.

2 — Poorly

The learner must actively recall what was said (or shown) in order to connect it with the other stream. Working memory is strained, and the learner is aware of the mismatch. Integration is no longer automatic and learning is impaired.

1 — Very poorly

The visual does not meaningfully support the narration at this moment. The learner experiences split attention and cannot reliably integrate the two streams; the pairing has failed pedagogically.

Note for the fresh-video set: if the relevant visual does not appear in the viewing window, or appears so far from the narration that it no longer feels connected, score the support low (1 or 2).
