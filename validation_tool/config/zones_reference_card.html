Zone Reference Card — Temporal Contiguity

Rate each CPIP using the zone whose cognitive description best fits what the learner experiences. Do not count seconds — judge the cognitive effort the learner must exert to connect the visual and the narration.

Optimal

The visual and narration are perceived as happening together. The learner does not notice any gap and does not need to exert any effort to link what is being said with what is being shown. Integration feels automatic and effortless.

Suboptimal

A small perceptual gap is noticeable, but the learner can bridge it using short-term memory without losing comprehension. The pairing still works pedagogically, but the learner must hold the narration (or the visual) briefly in mind while waiting for its partner to appear.

Disruptive

The learner must actively try to recall what was said (or shown) in order to connect it with its partner. Working memory is strained. The learner is aware of the mismatch and has to make a deliberate mental effort — integration is no longer automatic and the learning is impaired.

Unacceptable

By the time the partner (visual or narration) arrives, the learner has lost the connection entirely — attention has moved on, or the earlier content has faded from working memory. The learner experiences split attention and cannot reliably integrate the two streams. The pairing has failed.

Reminder: Choose the zone before estimating the delay in seconds — this keeps your perceptual judgement independent of any numerical estimate.
